1. Phrases

A phrase is a group of related words that works together as a unit but does not contain a complete subject-verb combination.

Types of Phrases

Noun Phrase

A group of words with a noun as its head.

Example: The intelligent student.

Verb Phrase

A phrase containing a main verb and sometimes auxiliary verbs.

Example: Is studying English.

Adjective Phrase

A phrase with an adjective as its head.

Example: Very beautiful.

Adverb Phrase

A phrase with an adverb as its head.

Example: Very slowly.

Prepositional Phrase

A phrase beginning with a preposition.

Example: In the classroom.

The Head of a Phrase

The head is the most important word in a phrase. It determines the type of the phrase.

Example: The beautiful girl. Head: girl.

2. Clauses

A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a verb.

Types of Clauses

Independent Clause

An independent clause expresses a complete thought.

Example: I study English.

Dependent Clause

A dependent clause does not express a complete thought.

Example: Because I was tired.

Types of Dependent Clauses

Noun Clause

It functions as a noun.

Example: I know that she is intelligent.

Adjective Clause

It describes a noun or pronoun.

Example: The girl who speaks English is my sister.

Adverb Clause

It gives information about time, reason, or condition.

Example: I stayed home because I was sick.

3. Difference Between Phrases and Clauses

Phrase

In the classroom.

It does not contain a complete subject-verb combination.

Clause

When I entered the classroom.

It contains a subject and a verb.
